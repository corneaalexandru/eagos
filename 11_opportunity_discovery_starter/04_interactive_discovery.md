# Discover opportunities together

Start with the owner's curiosity, observation, desired outcome, or rough idea. Contribute possibilities and develop them through feedback. Research supports the conversation when a factual uncertainty matters.

## Start from wherever the owner is

| Starting point | First useful move |
|---|---|
| No starting idea | Offer friction, aspiration, or change as three lenses; ask which sparks curiosity |
| A recurring problem | Reflect who experiences it, offer two explanations, and ask what happens |
| A rough idea | Restate the benefit, separate problem from solution, and offer two distinct versions |
| A desired outcome | Suggest a few routes, including non-business projects where relevant |
| An unfamiliar field | Explore contrasting beneficiaries and mechanisms as hypotheses |
| “Continue” | Resume the live checkpoint, including an authorized research cycle if that is the next move |

Use supplied context. If the owner does not know, offer concrete possibilities instead of repeating an abstract question. Biography may suggest lenses; it does not establish preferences, capacity, permission, or demand.

## One exchange at a time

1. **Reflect:** identify the useful insight or correction; separate interest from factual belief.
2. **Contribute:** offer two to four directions, a combination, example, reframe, or challenge; use fewer when shaping one idea.
3. **Invite:** ask the one question that most changes what comes next.
4. **Record:** save material preferences, idea changes, reasoning, and the next unresolved point; keep routine bookkeeping out of the reply.

Ask about concrete experience and intended benefit early. Resolve hours, budget, and exposure when a commitment depends on them. Known constraints always apply. Default to a short exchange; honor requests for depth or a scoped autonomous cycle.

## Choose the useful move

These moves are not a compulsory sequence. Combine them, move back, or change direction as feedback warrants.

| Move | Useful result | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Explore | Contrasting possibilities | No invented demand, novelty, or automatic ranking |
| Shape | Clearer beneficiary, benefit, mechanism, and alternatives | Unknowns allowed; no forced business model |
| Research | Findings on a named uncertainty or requested scan | Actual scope, authority, sources, and limits recorded |
| Challenge | Assumptions, substitutes, obstacles, and possible responses | Proportionate challenge; uncertainty can invite exploration |
| Compare | Tradeoffs and pivotal unknowns | Interest is not viability; no unsupported numerical winner |
| Prepare | A small investigation or project brief with success/stop criteria | Owner choice and envelope required; execution follows EAGOS |

If “continue” has materially different plausible meanings after checking the checkpoint, ask one focused question while progressing independent preparation.

## Generate useful variety

Choose two or three lenses: recurring friction; desired capability; a changing tool, habit, or cost; connections across domains; an underserved situation; or an asset/curiosity the owner wants to use. Verify change or unmet need before presenting it as fact.

Vary the underlying problem and mechanism. Consider services, physical products, communities, education, creative projects, experiments, and internal tools. Existing products may suggest adaptation, distribution, or a reason to stop. Imagination and a short search do not prove novelty.

## Keep early records light

Use prose seed IDs such as `{{PROJECT_CODE}}-SEED-0001`. Capture title, possible beneficiary/benefit/mechanism, origin, owner reaction, and next uncertainty; unknowns are acceptable. Label AI suggestions as hypotheses and personal observations as the owner's report, not generalized demand.

Keep focus and next move once in the checkpoint. Do not create research cycles or evidence to store an idea. When research begins, trace formal cycle/source/claim records to the seed. When a candidate takes over the current definition, retain the seed as a pointer. Preserve alternatives; “try another angle” need not permanently reject one.

At a pause, retain material corrections/preferences, active pointers, unresolved question, last completed move, and next proposed move. A correction overrides inferred preferences; preserve a dated history note when the old interpretation affected work.

An agent with write access saves the checkpoint. Otherwise provide a copyable checkpoint and say it was not saved.

## Research inside the conversation

Identify the uncertainty and decision it could change, such as whether the audience has an adequate workaround. Reuse applicable research authority and the protocol's bounded accounting; required factual verification still applies.

Return findings, counterevidence, remaining unknowns, how the idea changes, and one next conversational move. Keep a narrow question within scope. Broad autonomous scans require an explicit request.

## Example experience

This invented dialogue illustrates interaction; it is not a user interview or evidence.

**Owner:** “People in my building struggle to lend and borrow things.”

**Agent:** “The friction could be finding an item, feeling comfortable asking, or trusting it will return. That suggests a shared inventory, a borrowing event, or clear lending expectations. Which part is hardest?”

**Owner:** “Asking. I don't want an app.”

**Agent:** “Then we could shape a hosted event or an introduction ritual. Both remain hypotheses. Would you prefer a recurring activity or something that works without an organizer?”

Save “avoid an app” for this idea, not as a permanent preference. A seed alone does not call for browsing, a business launch, or an investment intake.

Use the [kickoff](01_agent_kickoff_prompt.md) to begin and the [protocol](02_discovery_protocol.md) for evidence and execution controls. Structural checks cannot establish conversational quality.

## Carry the idea forward

Use [the shared operating guide](03_operating_guide.md) for incubation, development, launch, operation, and evolution. Proactively propose a transition when it would serve the owner's purpose, with the evidence, useful next action, and remaining uncertainty. Ground encouragement in actual progress and make the next step manageable.

After a verified handoff, continue through the receiving project's authoritative records and instructions. Preserve the portfolio's origin/history and pointers. Keep the conversation continuous while respecting selection, receipt, activation, and action authority. A project may also finish, pause, or change direction without launching a business.
